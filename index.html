Call for papers, artist talks, and demos

Digital Humanities 3.0

AI, Distributed Networks, and the Post-Internet

Thursday, May 20, 2027
Mercy University, Manhattan Campus
47 West 34th Street, New York (Herald Square)

This symposium invites humanities scholars, artists, inventors and builders to NYC to explore how emerging technologies are forcing us to rethink and allowing us to reshape our academic, scholarly and educational practices and the infrastructures through which we produce, store, govern, and share information. Decentralized AI, blockchains and immutable networks that run on renewable energy, open-source hardware, quantum computing, and other evolving tools that incentivize the expanded use of clean, electric systems, offer an opportunity to move beyond centralized systems and institutional gatekeepers toward more open, participatory and collectively governed forms of knowledge creation, preservation and dissemination. At the same time, these technologies raise fundamental questions about the political economy of knowledge, including ownership, privacy, surveillance, authorship, expertise, authority, and power.

Come join us in building the new Web3 Digital Humanities.

Possible topics

  • Decentralized and locally run AI in research, teaching, and the archive
  • Blockchains, timestamping, and onchain publishing
  • The energy of knowledge: renewable networks and computation
  • Open-source hardware, self-hosting, and repair as scholarly infrastructure
  • Quantum computing, cryptography, and the long-term survival of records
  • Knowledge commons: peer review, credit, and collective governance
  • Authorship, ownership, and intellectual property after generative AI
  • Privacy, surveillance, and the political economy of platforms
  • Expertise and authority with permissionless tech
  • Artistic and curatorial practice on distributed networks
